Bar Pricing Review

One consistent way to price everything the bar pours, built on what most US bars already do. It is a starting point for Andrew, Nathan, Caleb and the bar manager to settle together. Beer costs come from our Oct 2 invoice; liquor and wine costs are estimates until we have invoices for them.

THE RECOMMENDATION

In seven lines

  • Pour 1.5 oz. Shots, highballs and the base spirit in cocktails get 1.5 oz; neat or rocks 2 oz; a double 3 oz; wine 5 oz. The bar manager signs off. We could not find Outback's spec in public, so his word on it decides.
  • Draft is the one real problem. At our invoice keg costs, a $5 16 oz runs a 30–36% pour cost, and up to 40% with the Insiders+ discount. Bars aim for about 20%. Proposal: $7 for 16 oz, $9 for 20 oz, one price for every tap. No 12 oz, no pitcher.
  • Cans get two prices, not one. Bud, Bud Light and the non-alcoholic cans stay $4. Space Dust costs us $1.58 a can, so it moves to $6.
  • Liquor is priced well. Keep it. Shots at $5 / $7 / $9 and the cocktail menu run about 9–18% pour cost, under 20% even after the Insiders+ 10%. One tidy-up: the Manhattan to $10, to match the Old Fashioned.
  • One register rule for anything off the menu. Serve plus level: shot $5, highball $8, neat or rocks $7, off-menu cocktail $10; call +$2, premium +$4. A double adds a second shot at $1 off.
  • Wine waits for invoices. If our Missouri wines cost about $10 a bottle, $6 a glass and $25 a bottle fit the usual target. If they cost $7, today's $5 / $18 already works.
  • Whole dollars, one price sheet. Prices stay whole dollars before tax. Once the team decides, every price goes into one Back office price sheet you can change any time. Discounts on alcohol stay on the list of things to check.

2 · How bars price

Pour cost, the one number bars watch

Pour cost is what the drink costs us, as a share of its price. A 20% pour cost means 20¢ of every dollar pays for what's in the glass. The other 80¢ covers staff, rent, glassware, spills and profit.

CategoryUsual target pour costWhy it differs
Liquor (shots, highballs, cocktails)15–20%A bottle goes a long way, so liquor carries the bar.
Draft beer15–20%Keg beer is cheap per ounce. Craft and import kegs run higher.
Bottled or canned beer24–28%Guests know what a can costs at the store.
Wine30–40%Some guides allow 22–45%. Bars usually take less margin on a bottle than on a glass, because a bottle sells all at once.
Whole bar, blended18–24%Depends on the mix of what sells.

Targets from BevSpot, Sculpture Hospitality and Backbar, seen Oct 4, 2026. The guides differ by a few points; these are the common ranges.

The formula

Price = cost of the pour ÷ target pour cost
Worked example with a real cost: a sixth-barrel of Stella costs us $74 and holds 661 oz.
$74 ÷ 661 = $0.112 per oz → a 16 oz glass costs $1.79.
At a 20% target: $1.79 ÷ 0.20 = $8.95 → round up to $9.
Today we charge $5, which is a 36% pour cost.

The formula gives the lowest price that hits the target. The market and the venue set the menu price, as long as it stays at or under target. For liquor our menu prices are already well above the formula. For draft they are well below it. That's why draft is the one place this review asks for a real change.

Rounding. Round up to a clean price point. We suggest whole dollars: easy to read on a menu, easy on tabs and tips. Our sales tax (8.725%) is added on top, so the guest pays odd cents either way.

The Insiders+ discount. Insiders+ members get 10% off the whole order, alcohol included. That raises the pour cost on their drinks by about a tenth: a 20% drink becomes 22%. The ladder below shows both.

3 · Spirit levels

Well, call, premium, and above

Bars sort every bottle into a level. The level sets the upcharge, so a guest who names a brand pays for it the same way on every drink. We already price shots this way: $5, $7, $9.

The double rule

A double is twice the liquor, not twice the drink. The glass, ice and mixer are already paid for. So a double adds a second shot at $1 off: well +$4, call +$6, premium +$8. A flat +$4 on every level would give the premium pour away at about a 38% pour cost.

Neat or rocks

No mixer, so the pour is 2 oz instead of 1.5. That's the shot price plus $2: well $7, call $9, premium $11. A drink served up, martini-style, rings as an off-menu cocktail (section 4).

Mixers

Soda-gun mixers cost pennies, so bars include them: cola, diet, lemon-lime, ginger ale, tonic, soda water, sour. Same for house juices and cream. Canned mixers cost real money: ginger beer swap +$1, energy drink +$2.

The level names and the idea of a fixed upcharge per level are standard across bar registers (see section 4). The dollar amounts are ours; call +$2 and premium +$4 match the shot prices on our menu today. Mixer charges are common practice, not from a source; the bar manager confirms.

4 · Drinks not on the menu

What bars and bar registers actually do

Andrew floated two ideas: (a) a price from how hard the drink is plus its cost, and (b) an itemized price, so much per pour plus the mixer. Bars use both, in different places. Idea (b) is what the register does on every order. Idea (a) is what a manager does once, when deciding a menu price.

The quick fix

The bar manager or an owner checks five prices at Outback Joplin, Chili's, Applebee's, Club 609 and Bricktown Brewery: domestic pint, craft or import pint, well drink, glass of house wine, margarita. One evening fills the gaps.

What it tells us

Our taps are craft and import. Outback reportedly charges about $7 for a 16 oz Stella, and big-city cinema bars charge $7 pints and $14–21 cocktails. A $7 pint and $8–10 cocktails sit above a Joplin happy hour and well under a city cinema. That fits a small-town cinema lounge.

6 · Draft price ladder

Draft at our real keg costs

All six taps are sixth-barrel kegs (661 oz) from the Missouri Eagle invoice of Oct 2, 2026. They cost within $12.50 of each other, so one price for every tap stays simple and fair.

TapKeg (invoice)Per oz16 oz cost16 oz at $5 (now)16 oz at $720 oz cost20 oz at $7 (now)20 oz at $9

Cost per ounce assumes every ounce in the keg sells. Foam, spills and line cleaning use some, so real pour cost runs a few points higher. With the Insiders+ 10% off, a $7 16 oz runs 24–28%; a $5 one runs 33–40%.

A 12 oz pour?

Not needed. 16 oz is the normal US draft glass and our base. A 12 oz mostly makes sense for very strong beers, or as a cheaper entry price. The one strong beer on tap, Space Dust (8.2%), is a question for the bar manager below.

A pitcher?

Not now. A 60 oz pitcher is 9% of a sixth-barrel, so kegs would run out faster. Pitchers are usually discounted, which our draft margin can't take yet. If groups ask, price it as 3.75 glasses at the 16 oz price, with no real discount: $26.

The gentler option

If $7 / $9 feels like too big a jump from $5 / $7, $6 / $8 is a middle step: 25–30% pour cost at 16 oz, 23–28% at 20 oz. Still above target, but a real improvement.

7 · Things to check

Questions, not answers

These need a check with the Missouri Division of Alcohol and Tobacco Control, the City of Joplin, or our accountant before we lean on them.

Drink specials and happy hour

  • Do Missouri or Joplin rules limit happy hours, 2-for-1s, or drink discounts by time of day?
  • Does a can bucket or a pitcher count as a special, and are there limits on serving one to a single guest?
  • Are there rules on how we advertise drink prices or specials?

Member discounts on alcohol

  • Insiders+ members get 10% off the whole order today, alcohol included. Is a percentage discount on alcohol for paying members allowed?
  • Do Insiders points earned on alcohol spend count as a discount on alcohol?
  • If either isn't allowed, can the perks apply to food, coffee, candy and tickets only?

Sales tax

  • Menu prices exclude tax today; 8.725% is added after discounts. Do we keep that, or move to tax-included whole-dollar prices?
  • If we move to tax-included prices, how does Missouri want the receipt to show the tax?

For the bar manager

  • Space Dust is 8.2%. A 20 oz is about 2.7 standard drinks. Should strong beers be 16 oz only?
  • What did Outback pour for a shot, a highball and a neat pour?
8 · Decisions for the team

What to settle at the meeting

  • Pour size. 1.5 oz standard, 2 oz neat, 3 oz double. Bar manager signs off.
  • Draft. $7 / $9 or $6 / $8, one price for every tap. No 12 oz, no pitcher.
  • Cans. $4 domestic and non-alcoholic; Space Dust $6 (or $5). Add a bucket of 4 domestic for $15, or not.
  • Level upcharges. Keep call +$2, premium +$4. Bottles above premium get their own price. Bar manager sorts every bottle into a level.
  • Double rule. A second shot at $1 off (+$4 / +$6 / +$8).
  • Off-menu rule. Serve + level + double + mixer, with the cost check as the manager's job.
  • Cocktails. Keep the menu prices; move the Manhattan to $10, or not.
  • Wine. Get wine invoices, then set the glass and bottle price with the calculator.
  • Rounding and tax. Whole dollars before tax, or tax-included prices.
  • Member pricing on alcohol. Keep the Insiders+ 10% on drinks while we check, or pause it on alcohol.

After the meeting. Whatever the team decides goes into one price sheet in Back office. You can change any price there, any time, without touching code. That sheet gets built after this review.
