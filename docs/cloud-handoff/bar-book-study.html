Royale Cinema Lounge · Bar study · Oct 4, 2026

The Royale Bar Book

A study of how the best bar registers work, what ours lacks, and a proposal: one bar screen you can read at a glance, color-coded by spirit, with drink icons drawn from each recipe, and a recipe book that knows what we can make tonight.

THE SCREEN

Everything on one page

Taps, cans, wine, cocktails and shots on one screen, no scrolling. Each drink's tile is tinted by its spirit, so the eye finds "the tequila ones" before reading.

THE ICONS

Drawn from the recipe

A small program draws each drink: the right glass, filled with its ingredients' colors, with its garnish on top. Staff pick up recipes without trying.

THE BOOK

Can we make it?

Hundreds of drinks A–Z, each with its recipe card and a live answer: yes, or "out of Cointreau". Filter to what we can make right now, and ring any of them up in one tap.

2 · The big chains

What Outback, Chili's and the rest do

Chains like Outback, Chili's, Applebee's, Buffalo Wild Wings, TGI Fridays and Olive Garden run enterprise registers (NCR Voyix Aloha, Oracle MICROS Simphony) or their own builds. Very little of how their bar screens look is public, so each point below is marked.

Public

Outback uses Aloha and MICROS

Outback's own bartender job postings ask for Aloha or MICROS experience and say drinks are made "following Outback Steakhouse recipes and standards".

Public

Tablets at the table

Chili's put Ziosk tablets on its tables and Applebee's rolled out E la Carte tablets, both widely covered in 2013–2014: guests reorder drinks and pay without waiting. Not re-checked for today.

Inferred

Chains standardize the spec

Every chain drink has one recipe, one glass and one garnish, shown to the bartender, so a margarita is the same in every state. The register enforces the recipe through fixed modifiers rather than free typing.

Inferred

Fixed price levels

Aloha and MICROS both sell time-based price levels; chains use them for happy hour and late-night menus that switch on by themselves.

Secondhand

Outback's drink library

Andrew heard Outback's bar screen has hundreds of drinks A–Z, each with its recipe card, and can show what can be made with what's in stock. We couldn't confirm this publicly. Consumer apps do exactly this (Jigger, Drinkshelf, Muddled, Bar Assistant), so it's very buildable.

3 · Our register

What the Alcohol tab lacks today

Hard to read at a glance

Beer, wine, cocktails and shots are separate lists you scroll through, and the buttons look alike. The fix is visual: color and shape, more than fewer screens.

No way to ring a call drink

"Tito's and soda, double" has no button. Staff either guess an item or type a custom one, which skips stock and the price checks.

Sizes and specials are missing

One price per beer kind; no pint, pitcher or bucket; no happy hour. Andrew's pending prices need sizes to exist first.

Recipes don't do anything yet

Recipes, ingredients, costs, counts and Ran out already exist, but a sale doesn't use them: nothing says a drink can't be made, and stock never goes down by itself.

5 · Headline feature

The Bar Book: can we make it?

Every drink the bar could make, A–Z, each with its recipe card. Each card checks our ingredients against what's in stock and answers plainly: can make, missing something, or ran out. "Makeable now" filters the book to tonight's possibilities, and "Uses what we have" sorts drinks by how much of our current stock they use. One tap rings the drink up with the right price and choices.

Linked to stock we already keep

Each ingredient is linked to its par-sheet line. When someone marks a bottle as ran out, every drink that needs it turns to "can't make" at once, and the shopping list and the ran-out email to Nathan and Mary work as today. No second inventory.

Not empty on day one

A seed list of about 150 well-known drinks (the classic cocktail canon plus common bar calls like a vodka soda), written in our own words, with glass, method and garnish. Our own recipes and house drinks sit on top and win.

Off-menu drinks ring correctly

A book drink that isn't on the menu rings at a set price by type (standard cocktail, premium cocktail, spirit and mixer) plus the tier, so the price check still works and nobody types a price.

6 · Drink icons

Icons drawn from the recipe

Every icon below is drawn by the same small program from the recipe alone: pick the glass, stack the ingredients from the bottom in their colors and in proportion, add ice, then the garnish. Nothing is hand-drawn, so a new drink gets its icon the moment its recipe is saved. The spirit colors are fixed, the same in light and dark, so staff learn one code.

Legible at button size

The same margarita at the register's tile size, a small chip size and the order-line size. The glass outline follows the theme; the drink colors stay the same.

7 · Under the hood

One set of recipe data for the book, the icons and the stock

Ingredients (exists, gains a few fields)

ingredients already has name, unit, bottle size, cost and its par-sheet link. Add a kind (spirit, liqueur, mixer, juice, syrup, bitters, garnish), a family for spirits (whiskey, rum, gin, vodka, tequila) that drives the colors, a tier (well, call, premium) and the brand.

Recipes become the book

recipes today belongs to a menu item. Let a recipe stand alone with its own name, so the book can hold drinks we don't sell yet. Add glass, method (build, shake, stir, blend), ice, garnishes, a price type and where it came from (seed list, house, menu).

"Can we make it?"

Worked out live, never stored: a drink is makeable when none of its required ingredients has an open Ran out and, where a count exists, the count isn't zero. Optional ingredients (a garnish) never block it, but show as "no orange peel".

Stock that moves

Later phase: each sale takes its recipe's ounces out of the expected stock, so the next count shows what's missing (the variance) and the par sheet knows when a bottle is nearly gone.

Migrations go to the local agent to apply. They mostly add columns (no grants needed). Any new table or function ships with its grants, per the Oct 30 rule.

8 · Plan

Four phases, each useful on its own

1

The look · M

The one-screen Bar tab, spirit tints and the icon generator for today's drinks. One migration adding the ingredient family. Register change, ships after close.

2

Pour choices · M

Tiers, serve, rim and double on cocktails and spirits; spirit-and-mixer drinks; beer sizes, pitchers and buckets; another round. Needs Andrew's prices and pour size.

3

The Bar Book · L

A–Z book, recipe cards, can-we-make with the Ran out link, filters and sort, one-tap ring-up, and the seed list of about 150 drinks. Two migrations.

4

Stock that moves · M

Sales take ounces out of expected stock; counts show the variance; the par sheet warns before a bottle runs out. Happy-hour pricing can ride along here.
